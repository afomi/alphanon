18 Dimensions

Walter Russell & Eric Dollard

Walter Russell's Dimensional Model

Russell conceived the universe as a system of 18 dimensions: 9 positive (centripetal/compressive) and 9 negative (centrifugal/expansive). These dimensions represent the rhythmic interchange between gravity and radiation.

9 Positive Dimensions (Compression)

Inward spiraling toward stillness at the center:

  • +1: Point of beginning motion
  • +2: Line of electric potential
  • +3: Plane of magnetic field
  • +4: Sphere of incandescence
  • +5: Cube of crystallization
  • +6: Sphere of maximum compression
  • +7: Reversal plane (amplitude)
  • +8: Sphere of radioactive decay
  • +9: Point of maximum stillness

9 Negative Dimensions (Expansion)

Outward spiraling toward space:

  • -1: Point of reversed motion
  • -2: Line of discharge
  • -3: Plane of dissolution
  • -4: Sphere of cooling
  • -5: Cube of dispersion
  • -6: Sphere of minimum compression
  • -7: Zero plane (rest)
  • -8: Sphere of regeneration
  • -9: Point of absolute stillness

Eric Dollard's Framework

Dollard describes three fundamental measures of electrical phenomena that map onto geometric dimensions:

Span / Distance (1D)

Linear extension in space. The reach or extent of a field. Corresponds to Russell's lines of force.

Density / Area (2D)

Concentration per unit area. The intensity of field distribution across a surface. Corresponds to Russell's planes.

Concentration / Volume (3D)

Mass per unit volume. The gathering of potential into form. Corresponds to Russell's spheres and cubes.

Synthesis

Both frameworks describe the same underlying reality: the rhythmic breathing of the universe between concentration and expansion, measured across 1D, 2D, and 3D space.
